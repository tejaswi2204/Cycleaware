About this App
What it does
01
What does CycleAware predict?
It predicts two things — whether your next cycle will start with spotting, and if so, how many days before bleeding begins. This helps you prepare in advance and never be caught off guard.
02
How is it different from other period apps?
Most apps predict when your period starts. CycleAware goes deeper — it predicts the spotting-to-bleeding gap, which varies from person to person and month to month based on stress, health, and history. No existing app addresses this specifically.
03
How does it work?
It uses a machine learning model trained on diverse cycle patterns across different ages, conditions, and lifestyles. The more accurately you fill in the details, the more personalised your prediction will be.
!
Important note
CycleAware is an indicative tool, not a medical device. Predictions are based on patterns and may not always be accurate. Always consult a doctor for medical concerns.
The 3 sections
I
Section 1 — About You
Your age, BMI, medical condition, contraceptive use and typical spotting gap. These are static details that form your biological baseline and do not change month to month.
II
Section 2 — This Cycle
How you have been feeling this month — stress, sleep, mood, hydration, pain, activity level, cycle length, and whether you have had any travel disruption or illness. These change every cycle and directly influence the prediction.
III
Section 3 — Your History
What happened in your last 1–2 cycles — whether spotting occurred, how long the gap was, how many months in a row you have had spotting, and your overall spotting rate. History is the strongest signal the model uses.
Understanding Features
Section 1 — About You
Bm
BMI (Body Mass Index)
▼
BMI measures your body weight relative to your height. It influences hormonal balance which affects cycle regularity and spotting patterns. Here is how to calculate it:
BMI = Weight (kg) ÷ Height² (m²)
Example: 60 kg ÷ (1.65 × 1.65) = 22.0

Pg
Personal Base Gap
▼
Your typical number of days between spotting starting and bleeding beginning. Think back over your last few cycles — if spotting usually lasts 3 days before flow starts, set this to 3. This is the single strongest predictor in the model.
Mc
Medical Condition
▼
Select any diagnosed cycle-related condition. PCOS and endometriosis significantly increase spotting probability and can affect gap length. Thyroid disorders also influence hormonal regulation. Select None if you have no diagnosed condition.
Cv
Contraceptive
▼
Certain contraceptives affect spotting. The pill and implant tend to reduce spotting probability by regulating hormones. The IUD (especially non-hormonal) can sometimes increase irregular spotting. Select None if you do not use any.
Section 2 — This Cycle
Cl
Cycle Length
▼
The number of days from the first day of your last period to the first day of your current period. A typical cycle is 21–35 days. When your cycle length deviates significantly from your usual average, spotting is more likely to occur.
St
Stress Level
▼
Rate how stressed you have been this month on a scale of 1 to 10. High stress (7–10) raises cortisol which disrupts estrogen and progesterone — this often shortens the spotting-to-bleeding gap and increases the likelihood of spotting occurring.
Sl
Sleep Quality
▼
Rate your average sleep quality this month from 1 (very poor) to 10 (excellent). Poor sleep is linked to hormonal imbalance. Sleep and stress are closely connected — low sleep often means high stress, which in turn affects your cycle.
Md
Mood Score
▼
Rate your overall mood this month from 1 (very low) to 10 (very positive). Mood is an indirect signal of hormonal balance and stress levels. Consistently low mood often correlates with hormonal shifts that affect cycle patterns.
Hy
Hydration Level
▼
Rate how well hydrated you have been this month from 1 (very dehydrated) to 10 (well hydrated). Hydration affects circulation and body regulation. Higher activity levels typically require higher hydration.
Ac
Activity Level
▼
How physically active have you been this month? Low means mostly sedentary, Moderate means regular light exercise, High means intense or frequent workouts. High activity generally helps regulate cycles, but extreme exercise can disrupt them.
Section 3 — Your History
Sr
Personal Spotting Rate
▼
How often do you get spotting across your cycles? If spotting happens in 3 out of every 5 months, your rate is 0.60. If it almost never happens, set it closer to 0. If it almost always happens, set it closer to 1.
Gv
Gap Variability
▼
How consistent is your spotting-to-bleeding gap across months? If your gap is always the same — set near 0. If it changes significantly each month — set near 1. Higher variability means predictions are slightly less certain.
Cs
Consecutive Spotting Months
▼
How many months in a row have you had spotting before this cycle? If last month had spotting and the month before also did, enter 2. This streak pattern is an important signal — long streaks slightly reduce the probability of spotting continuing indefinitely.
